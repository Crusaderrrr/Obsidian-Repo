**Irezumi** (入れ墨, literally "**inserting ink**") is Japan's traditional tattoo art — one of the most visually rich and symbolically dense tattoo styles in the world.

## History 
It emerged in Japan during the Edo period (roughly 1600s–1800s), primarily in cities like Edo (modern Tokyo) and Osaka. The tattoo was made by a wooden/bamboo stick.

## Who wore it
Early irezumi had a complicated social status. It was used as **punishment marking for criminals**, but simultaneously developed as a mark of pride among laborers, firefighters, and craftsmen — people who worked shirtless and wanted to display both **toughness and belonging**. Later it became deeply associated with the **yakuza**, which led to decades of stigma in Japan that still lingers today.

## Style
The style is defined by a few core elements: large-scale compositions that wrap the body (often full back pieces, sleeves, bodysuits), subjects drawn from Japanese **mythology** and **folklore** — dragons, koi, phoenixes, oni, tigers, cherry blossoms, waves — and a specific approach to shading using gradients called _bokashi_. **Everything is intentionally symbolic**.

## Conclusion
It's a style built around narrative and symbolism more than decoration — which fits your dragon/oni concept well.
